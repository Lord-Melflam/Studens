\begin{why}
Two properties of this picture are decisions rather than defaults, and both are
argued in \texttt{architecture-style.md}.

\textbf{One deployable, not several.} Microservices were excluded by two
independent arguments, and the decisive one is in section~\ref{sec:kernel}: the
mechanism that makes distributed consistency possible is the mechanism the
privacy design exists to prevent.

\textbf{One database, three schemas, three roles.} This is what turns FR-B3 from
a convention into something the database refuses. A lint rule catches an import;
it cannot catch a module issuing SQL against another module's tables, because
that is a string.
\end{why}

\section{Where the boundary is actually enforced}

Three mechanisms, deliberately overlapping, in increasing order of strength.
The point of listing them together is that each catches what the one above it
cannot.

\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[x=1cm,y=1cm]

\node[wbox={13.6cm}{1.25cm}] (a) at (0,2.0)
  {\textbf{1. Le manifeste du paquet} \tiny \texttt{package.json}\\[0.4mm]
   \tiny Un paquet qui ne d\'eclare pas une d\'ependance ne peut pas la r\'esoudre. V\'erifi\'e par \texttt{boundaries.test.ts}: les d\'ependances descendent, jamais l'inverse.};

\node[wbox={13.6cm}{1.25cm}] (b) at (0,0.35)
  {\textbf{2. La r\`egle de lint} \tiny \texttt{no-restricted-imports}\\[0.4mm]
   \tiny Attrape ce qu'un manifeste ne voit pas: un import profond vers \texttt{@studens/x/src/...} se r\'esout tr\`es bien et casse quand m\^eme la fronti\`ere.};

\node[wtier={13.6cm}{1.25cm}] (c) at (0,-1.3)
  {\textbf{3. Les droits SQL} \tiny \texttt{GRANT} / absence de \texttt{GRANT}\\[0.4mm]
   \tiny Attrape ce qu'aucun outil JavaScript ne voit: une requ\^ete SQL vers la table d'un autre module. C'est une cha\^ine de caract\`eres, donc rien c\^ot\'e code ne peut la lire.};

\draw[flow] (a) -- (b);
\draw[flow] (b) -- (c);

\node[cell={3.2cm}{\sffamily\tiny\color{muted}}] at (7.4,1.9) {d\'etecte\\\`a la r\'esolution};
\node[cell={3.2cm}{\sffamily\tiny\color{muted}}] at (7.4,0.25) {d\'etecte\\\`a la compilation};
\node[cell={3.2cm}{\sffamily\tiny\bfseries\color{studens}}] at (7.4,-1.4) {\textbf{refuse}\\\`a l'ex\'ecution};
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}

The third is different in kind from the first two. They \emph{detect}; it
\emph{refuses}. A developer who bypasses the first two, or a bug that constructs
a query at runtime, still hits a role with no grant.

\begin{careful}
The first two live in the repository and can be edited by whoever is changing
the code. The third lives in the database and is changed by a migration, which
is reviewed like anything else but takes a deliberate act.

That asymmetry is the reason FR-B15 exists: the gates must hold regardless of
author, including the project owner, because with a team of one, review of your
own code is self-review.
\end{careful}

\subsection{Why cross-schema references are plain ids}

Every reference \emph{inside} a schema is a real foreign key. Every reference
\emph{across} schemas is a plain string column with an index and no constraint.
That is deliberate and it costs something, so it is worth stating why.

\begin{center}
\sffamily\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{4.6cm}p{9.4cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{What a foreign key would give} & \textbf{What it would cost} \\
\midrule
Referential integrity enforced by the database & A Prisma relation must be declared on \emph{both} sides, so \texttt{ryc} referencing \texttt{platform.Member} forces a back-relation on \texttt{Member}. Tier~1 would then depend on tier~3, inverting FR-B10 \\[2mm]
Cascading deletes & Which is the wrong behaviour anyway: FR-C9 makes contributions permanent, and nothing may cascade into them \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

A plain id also survives module extraction unchanged, which is the property
FR-B10 and FR-B11 exist to preserve. The cost is real and recorded at the top of
\texttt{prisma/schema.prisma}: Prisma will not enforce integrity across schemas,
so a review can point at a course that no longer exists. FR-D16 turns that from
a bug into intended behaviour, because a review of a retired course must
survive.

\section{The grant matrix}

The security boundary, as the database actually holds it. Read live on
2026-09-10 from \texttt{information\_schema.role\_table\_grants}.

\begin{center}
\sffamily\scriptsize
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.25}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{4.7cm}p{2.9cm}p{2.9cm}p{2.9cm}@{}}
\toprule
& \textbf{\color{studens}studens\_platform} & \textbf{studens\_ref} & \textbf{studens\_ryc} \\
\midrule
\texttt{platform.*} & all & \textcolor{muted}{none} & \textcolor{muted}{none} \\
\texttt{ref.*} & \textcolor{muted}{none} & all & SELECT \\
\texttt{ryc.ReviewAttributed} & \textcolor{muted}{none} & \textcolor{muted}{none} & all \\
\texttt{ryc.ReviewImported} & \textcolor{muted}{none} & \textcolor{muted}{none} & all \\
\rowcolor{anonlt}
\texttt{ryc.ReviewAnonymous} & \textbf{INSERT, UPDATE, SELECT} & \textcolor{muted}{none} & \textbf{SELECT only} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Four things are readable directly off that table, and the last one is the point
of the whole design.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1.2mm]
\item \textbf{A feature module cannot see platform data.} No grant on
  \texttt{platform.*} for \texttt{studens\_ryc}, so member records, sessions,
  the audit log and above all the quota counter are simply not reachable.
\item \textbf{The catalogue is read-only to feature modules.} Only ingestion
  writes it.
\item \textbf{The platform cannot read a feature module's own data.} The arrow
  does not point the other way either: no grant on
  \texttt{ryc.ReviewAttributed}.
\item \textbf{Nobody holds DELETE on \texttt{ryc.ReviewAnonymous}.} FR-C9 makes
  anonymous contributions permanent, and FR-C10's removal is a status change.
  The absence of that grant is the enforcement.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{careful}
\texttt{studens\_platform} also holds full rights on
\texttt{platform.\_prisma\_migrations}, picked up from
\texttt{ALTER DEFAULT PRIVILEGES}. Harmless, since migrations run as the owner,
but it is an unintended grant and unintended grants are how matrices rot.
Worth a narrowing migration.
\end{careful}

\subsection{This matrix is tested, not just documented}

\texttt{scripts/verify-isolation.sql} asserts fourteen properties: every grant
above, and more importantly every \emph{absence}. It is run by
\texttt{npm run gates:db} and by a CI job with a PostgreSQL service.

Each check asserts which role it is actually running as before doing anything,
because the first version of that script passed while proving nothing: every
\texttt{SET ROLE} was itself denied, so all fourteen checks ran as the table
owner with full access and each ``must fail'' case quietly succeeded. See
\texttt{LESSONS.md} section~1.

\section{The anonymity kernel}\label{sec:kernel}

The one piece of this system that is genuinely hard, and the reason the
architecture is what it is.

\subsection{The problem in one line}

FR-C4 requires a per-person contribution limit. FR-C2 forbids storing any member
identifier on an anonymous contribution. Enforcing a per-person limit appears to
require knowing which person.

\begin{why}
They are not contradictory, because they are facts about different objects.

\textbf{Counting is a fact about the member. Linking is a fact about the
contribution.} To enforce ``at most N per period'' the system needs to know
\emph{how many} a member has made, never \emph{which ones}.
\end{why}

\subsection{The write, drawn}

\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[x=1cm,y=1cm]

\foreach \i/\y/\n in {0/0/{navigateur}, 1/-1.05/{\texttt{apps/api}},
                      2/-2.25/{module \texttt{ryc}}, 3/-3.85/{noyau, dans \texttt{platform}},
                      4/-5.5/{PostgreSQL}} {
  \node[wbox={3.6cm}{0.6cm}] (l\i) at (-5.6,\y) {\n};
}

\node[wfork={8.4cm}{0.65cm}] (s1) at (1.6,0)
  {POST /api/reviews \tiny · le formulaire, et le choix de la voie};
\node[wbox={8.4cm}{0.65cm}] (s2) at (1.6,-1.05)
  {le module valide le cours, la note, le texte};
\node[wbox={8.4cm}{0.95cm}] (s3) at (1.6,-2.25)
  {le module demande au noyau de publier\\[0.3mm]
   \tiny il ne peut pas \'ecrire lui-m\^eme: pas de droit INSERT};
\node[wtier={8.4cm}{1.75cm}] (s4) at (1.6,-3.85)
  {\textbf{une seule transaction}\\[0.6mm]
   \tiny 1. \texttt{UPDATE MemberQuota SET used = used + 1}\\[0.2mm]
   \tiny \phantom{1. } \texttt{WHERE memberId = ? AND used < limite}\\[0.3mm]
   \tiny 2. si 0 ligne touch\'ee: refus, rien n'est \'ecrit\\[0.3mm]
   \tiny 3. sinon \texttt{INSERT INTO ryc.ReviewAnonymous},\\[0.2mm]
   \tiny \phantom{3. } \textbf{sans aucune r\'ef\'erence au membre}};
\node[wbox={8.4cm}{0.65cm}] (s5) at (1.6,-5.5)
  {COMMIT \tiny · les deux \'ecritures, ou aucune};

\foreach \a/\b in {s1/s2, s2/s3, s3/s4, s4/s5} {\draw[flow] (\a)--(\b);}
\foreach \i in {0,1,2,3,4} {\draw[line,dashed] (l\i.east) -- ($(l\i.east)+(1.4,0)$);}
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}

\begin{why}
\textbf{Step 1 is a conditional update, not a read then a write.} An earlier
version of this design read the counter, compared it, then wrote. Two
submissions arriving together both read the same value and both pass, which is a
classic race and would have shipped. The single atomic
\texttt{UPDATE ... WHERE used < limit} makes the check and the increment one
operation.

\textbf{The module cannot perform step 3 itself.} \texttt{studens\_ryc} holds
SELECT and nothing else on the anonymous table. So the quota cannot be bypassed
by the module that owns the feature, and the exception is one grant rather than
an open door.

\textbf{Both writes are in one transaction and nothing links them.} That is the
whole argument against microservices: with a database per service this becomes a
saga, and a saga persists a correlation identifier tying the quota increment to
the contribution, which is exactly the pair FR-C2 forbids. \textbf{The
coordination mechanism would be the forbidden link.}
\end{why}

\subsection{What the kernel deliberately does not do}

It does not validate that the course exists. \texttt{studens\_platform} has no
grant on \texttt{ref} at all, so it could not if it wanted to.

That is the right division rather than an oversight. The module owns domain
knowledge, which is what a valid course is; the platform owns the quota and
unlinkability. The kernel writes an opaque target identifier it does not
interpret, and the module validates before asking. Discovered by the grants
refusing the lookup, which is a boundary doing its job.

\section{The ingestion pipeline}

Long, network-bound, and never in a request path. It runs in the worker.

\begin{tabular}{@{}p{3.3cm}p{5.4cm}p{5.4cm}@{}}
\toprule

\midrule

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

\begin{careful}
\textbf{Two rules in the loader are about reviews, not about the catalogue.}

Course ids must be \emph{stable} across runs, because reviews reference them and
cross-schema references are plain ids. Recreating courses with fresh identifiers
would silently orphan every review ever written. Courses are therefore matched
on code and keep the id they have.

And nothing is ever deleted from \texttt{ref.Course}. Codes vanish from the
catalogue routinely, and a review of a retired course must survive (FR-D16). A
course no longer offered simply stops gaining offerings.
\end{careful}

\begin{why}
A queue looks like infrastructure and is storage. If a queue entry carried a
submitter reference so the pipeline could sanction an author, unlinkability
would be broken \emph{in the queue} while the final table stayed clean, and
nobody would be looking there.

\textbf{A table rather than a message broker}, because a broker is another
process, another failure mode and another thing to operate on a two-core
machine, for a workload of a few hundred submissions. Postgres
\texttt{SELECT ... FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED} is enough and keeps the atomicity
guarantees the rest of the design already relies on.
\end{why}

\begin{careful}
\textbf{Backup cadence is a privacy parameter, not only an operational one.}

FR-C3 promises no linkage from any single stored snapshot. Across \emph{two}
snapshots taken within one quota window, the quota counter has moved while the
anonymous table has gained rows dated that day, which narrows authorship. The
bound is set by the backup interval and the quota window together, which is why
OPEN-41 sits against FR-C3 and FR-C12 rather than against NFR-O1 alone.

Whoever configures backups is making a privacy decision.
\end{careful}

\section{Open questions this document raises}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1.5mm]
\item \textbf{The unintended grant on \texttt{\_prisma\_migrations}} should be
  narrowed. Harmless today; unintended grants are how a matrix rots.

\item \textbf{There is no health check yet}, and Oracle's idle reclamation
  makes one mandatory rather than nice to have. It is the cheapest possible
  script and its absence is the largest operational risk in the system.

\item \textbf{Per-module database credentials do not exist in production
  form.} Locally one user is a member of all three roles, which is a testing
  affordance. The deployed shape is three credentials and three pools, so a
  connection cannot escalate at all. Nothing has been written for that.

\item \textbf{Nothing measures anything.} There are no metrics and no
  structured logs, and FR-B12 already constrains what a log may contain: no
  record may name a member and a target together. Designing observability after
  the fact, against that constraint, is harder than designing it now.
\end{enumerate}
