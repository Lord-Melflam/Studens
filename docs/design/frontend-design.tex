\section{Four principles, taken from the specification}

These are not aesthetic preferences. Each one is a requirement expressed as a
drawing rule, and each is testable by looking at a screen.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2mm]
\item \textbf{Nothing that does not exist is shown.} No greyed-out feature, no
  empty ratings panel, no ``coming soon''. A placeholder claims the platform
  does something it cannot, and goal~2 prefers a small app that genuinely
  works. Where a thing is absent, a sentence says so.

\item \textbf{The anonymity choice is a fork in the road, not a checkbox.}
  FR-C9 makes an anonymous contribution permanent and unrecoverable for its
  author, forever. A toggle beside a submit button is the wrong shape for a
  decision of that weight, so it gets its own step, its own colour, and its own
  confirmation (FR-C11).

\item \textbf{Numbers describe the course; prose discusses the teaching.}
  FR-D20. No screen anywhere shows a numeric or categorical rating attached to
  an identifiable person.

\item \textbf{The shell is not a module.} FR-B16 to FR-B18. The shell shows
  navigation and a module registry; it never learns what a course is. This is
  enforced by a test, and it should be visible in the drawings too.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{why}
Principle 2 is the one that shapes the most pixels. Every other student review
platform treats anonymity as a preference. Here it is irreversible, which makes
it closer to signing something than to ticking something.
\end{why}

The two contribution paths never share a colour, anywhere, at any size. A reader
scanning a course page can tell attributed from anonymous without reading a
word, and a contributor mid-submission can tell which path they are on at a
glance.

Both are chosen to be distinguishable from the blue chrome \emph{and} from each
other. That is why the rest of the palette is constrained rather than free: if
the primary moves, these two have to stay clear of it.

\subsection{Themes, and the one thing a theme may not touch}

A per-institution theme is planned: a student at UCLouvain would see UCLouvain's
blues, one at ULB would see ULB's. That is a good idea and it is cheap to keep
possible, provided one line is drawn now.

\begin{center}
\sffamily\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{4.6cm}p{4.4cm}p{5.0cm}@{}}
\toprule
& \textbf{Themeable} & \textbf{Why} \\
\midrule
Chrome: primary, accent, links, buttons, headers & \textbf{yes} & Decoration. Nothing depends on which blue it is \\[1.5mm]
Neutrals: ink, paper, lines & yes, within contrast limits & Same \\[1.5mm]
\textbf{The attributed and anonymous colours} & \textbf{never} & They carry meaning. A theme that recoloured them could make the two paths look alike, and that is the one thing the interface must never do \\[1.5mm]
Warning and danger & no & Also meaning, and a theme could make a warning look ordinary \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{why}
This is a 1.5-class decision: cheap now, expensive later. If the design tokens
ship as one flat set, a theme will recolour everything in it, including the two
colours the anonymity design leans on. Splitting them now costs nothing.

\textbf{Concretely:} two groups of CSS custom properties, one themeable and one
not, with the second declared where a theme cannot reach it. To be recorded as
an open question if the theming plan is confirmed.
\end{why}

\subsection{Institution marks: usable here, not shippable}

The marks above are reproduced in this draft to show what the product sits
beside. Two separate problems stand between that and shipping them.

\begin{center}
\sffamily\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{4.2cm}p{4.0cm}p{5.6cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Mark} & \textbf{Copyright, per Commons} & \textbf{What still blocks use} \\
\midrule
UCLouvain & Public domain & Trademark \\
ULB & Public domain & Trademark \\
EPL & CC BY-SA 4.0 & Trademark, plus share-alike in an MIT repository \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

``Public domain'' on Commons means below the threshold of originality for
\emph{copyright}. It says nothing about trademark, and trademark is the harder
problem.

This matters more now that the palette is institutional. Borrowing an
institution's colours is ordinary; reproducing its mark is a claim about who
you are.

\begin{openq}
\textbf{Proposed:} the product shows an institution's \emph{name} as text, never
its mark, until permission exists in writing. Logos stay out of the repository
(\texttt{scripts/fetch-logos.sh}, gitignored). A theme can match an
institution's colours without reproducing its mark.
\end{openq}

\begin{why}
Three properties are worth reading off the diagram.

\textbf{Arrows point down only.} The shell knows its modules; no module knows
the shell, and no module knows another module (FR-B10, FR-B18).

\textbf{No module talks to the database.} A module's browser code talks HTTP and
declares its own response types. \texttt{@studens/ref} reads files and is Node
only, so a browser reaching module storage would break FR-B11 whatever the
language.

\textbf{Adding a module is one line.} \texttt{apps/web/src/shell/registry.ts} is
the only file in the shell allowed to import a module package, which a test
enforces.
\end{why}

\section{Who uses it}

Three people, taken from the actors in \texttt{requirements.md}~2 and the module
specification in~3.4. They are not invented: each corresponds to a role the
requirements already name.

\begin{center}
\sffamily\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{3.5cm}p{5.4cm}p{5.4cm}@{}}
\toprule
& \textbf{What they want} & \textbf{What they fear} \\
\midrule
\textbf{The Researcher}\newline{\footnotesize a student building a PAE} &
To know which electives are worth taking before the deadline, and what each will
actually cost in hours &
Choosing a course that wrecks a quadrimester, and having no way to tell in
advance \\[2mm]
\textbf{The Contributor}\newline{\footnotesize a student or alumnus who took it} &
To warn or recommend, usefully and in their own words &
Being identified as the person who criticised a lecturer who will grade them or
their friends \\[2mm]
\textbf{The Moderator}\newline{\footnotesize AC-4} &
To remove what is defamatory or names a third party, quickly &
Having no way to act, or acting on the wrong thing \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{why}
The Contributor's fear is the entire reason FR-C exists. It is worth stating in
the persona rather than only in the requirements, because every drawing decision
about the fork traces back to it.
\end{why}

\begin{why}
Three things on screen B are requirements rather than taste.

\textbf{The aggregate carries its own denominator} (``d'apr\`es 14 avis'',
``11 r\'eponses''). FR-D10 and FR-D23: a number without its count invites being
quoted without it.

\textbf{The anonymous review shows a violet chip and nothing else about its
author.} No trust tier, no domain, no institution (FR-C16). The named one shows
a name because that is what attributed means.

\textbf{The assessment block is labelled ``fiche officielle''.} It is scraped
(FR-D19), so it is UCLouvain's claim and not a student's, and the interface
should not blur the two.
\end{why}

\begin{openq}
\textbf{Course titles do not fit.} Real ones run long: \emph{Software
Maintenance and Evolution}, \emph{Valorisation financi\`ere des engagements
actuariels}. Screen A truncates at roughly 27 characters, so every row keeps the
same height and the list stays scannable, which is the point of a list.

The alternative is variable-height rows, which reads worse and makes the
programme list harder to scan. Truncation is proposed rather than decided,
because it hides information and the full title is one tap away on the course
page.
\end{openq}

\end{tikzpicture}

Two things this scenario forces on the interface, both already requirements.

\textbf{The year is chosen, not assumed.} FR-D4: the academic year is the one
the reviewer \emph{took} the course, which here is three years before they are
typing. Defaulting it to the current year would silently mislabel most
contributions from alumni, who are a large share of the useful ones.

\textbf{A review can outlive its course.} FR-D16: codes change and courses
retire, so a review may name a year for which no catalogue entry exists. The
course page shows the review with its stated year and simply has no offering
context to show beside it.

\begin{careful}
This is also the scenario that makes the complement problem mild. A 2022 cohort
has dispersed, so the candidate set for an anonymous review of that year is both
large and hard to enumerate. The exposure concentrates on the \emph{current}
year, which is worth remembering when deciding how prominently to show recent
reviews.
\end{careful}

\begin{why}
The moderator's screen shows the review, its target and its date, and offers
removal. It shows \textbf{no author} for an anonymous review, because there is

That is not a missing feature to fill in later. Content can be removed; the
author cannot be reached, ever. The screen has to make that legible or a
moderator will look for the button that does not exist.

Removal is a \emph{status change} and never a delete (FR-C9), which is why no
role holds DELETE on the anonymous table.
\end{why}

\begin{why}
The red dashed line is the point of the drawing: it marks a join that is

writes as two requests with a shared correlation id, logging a submission event
with both the member and the course, or putting a draft id in browser storage
that also reaches the server, would each rebuild the link in a place nobody is
looking. FR-B12 covers the logging case; the others are matters of not doing
them.
\end{why}

\section{What this draft raises}

Recorded here rather than added to \texttt{requirements.md}, because none of it
is decided yet.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1.5mm]
\item \textbf{Institution marks are not shippable.} Copyright is the easy half;
  trademark is the hard one, and a university mark in a third-party student app
  implies an endorsement that does not exist. Proposed: the institution's
  \emph{name} as text until permission exists in writing.

\item \textbf{Long course titles do not fit a scannable list.} Truncation at
  roughly 27 characters is proposed. It hides information, and the alternative,
  variable-height rows, makes the list harder to scan.

\item \textbf{The language of the interface is not decided.} This draft is in
  French because the target users are francophone and their own document is
  French. But FR-D19 shows scraped text in whatever language the catalogue
  publishes it, and the vision includes Dutch-speaking institutions. So the
  interface language and the content language are two questions, not one.

\item \textbf{Nothing here is designed for a phone.} A student at the PAE
  deadline is plausibly on one. Every screen above assumes a wide viewport, and
  the two-column fork in particular does not survive a narrow one.

\item \textbf{The moderator's screens are sketched, not designed.} Scenario 4
  shows the flow, and FR-E has seven requirements with several still open
  (OPEN-7, OPEN-8, OPEN-24). Designing them before those close would be
  guessing.
\end{enumerate}
